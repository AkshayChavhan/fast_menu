Part one

Setting up

For the owner. From creating an account to a live menu with tables, codes and a team, in an afternoon.

1Welcome

fast_menu turns the QR code on your table into a digital menu that guests can order from, with a waiter always in the loop and one bill per table.

What it does

Guests scan a code and see your menu on their phone, with photos, allergens and prices that you change from your own phone. With ordering switched on, they build an order, and a waiter confirms it with one scan before it reaches the kitchen. The counter sees every open table and marks it paid once. Optionally, a tablet in the kitchen shows tickets, and guests are pointed to your Google reviews after they pay.

Who does what

Everyone signs in on the same page and lands in the screen made for their role. You create the logins for your staff yourself; nobody needs to register.

A service in five steps

GuestOrdersfrom the table code and shows a short code
WaiterScans and approvespicking the table if needed
KitchenCooksfrom the ticket, marks it ready
WaiterServesand edits or adds if guests ask
CounterMarks paidonce for the whole table

Two rules make this safe. Nothing reaches the kitchen or the bill until a member of staff approves it. And every price is checked again by the system when the order is placed, so a stale menu on someone's phone cannot produce a wrong bill.

2Your first day as an owner

Create the account, activate the free trial, fill in the basics, and decide when to go live.

Creating the account

  1. Open the sign-up page and enter your full name, email and a password of at least six characters. Tap Create account.
  2. If the screen says Check your email, open the confirmation link we sent, then sign in. If it says You're all set, tap Go to dashboard.

The email you sign up with is the owner login. Everyone else gets a login you create under Staff, so there is no need for a second account for the same restaurant.

Activating the free trial

Every restaurant gets fifteen days free, once. Before the dashboard opens for the first time you are asked to say which hotel or restaurant this is, on a page headed Activate your free 15-day trial.

FieldWhat to enter
Mobile numberThe restaurant's main contact number, with the country code picked from the list. If verification is on, a six-digit code is texted to it.
City and pincodeWhere the restaurant is. Used to tell two restaurants with similar names apart.
GSTINOptional. Fifteen characters, like 27AAPFU0939F1ZV. A quick way to prove this is a different business.

Tap Start my trial, or Send me the code and then Verify and start my trial. One of three things happens:

  • Your 15-day trial has started. The dashboard opens. The fifteen days count from this moment, not from sign-up.
  • We're checking your hotel. A restaurant with a very similar name already uses fast_menu in your area, so a person looks at it, usually within a day. You can build the whole menu meanwhile; only publishing waits.
  • This hotel already has an account. The trial was refused because this restaurant already used one. If you work there, ask its owner to add you under Staff instead. If you think this is a mistake, contact support.
Why the checks

The trial is per restaurant, not per email. A phone number or GSTIN that already activated a trial cannot activate another, and a look-alike name in the same pincode is reviewed by hand. This keeps the trial fair for everyone.

Restaurant details

Open Settings in the sidebar. The Restaurant details card holds:

  • Name and Description, shown at the top of the menu.
  • Public URL slug, the last part of your menu's web address. Letters, numbers and hyphens, and it has to be unique. Change it before you print codes, not after, because printed codes carry the address.
  • Currency, Default language and Languages offered, which add a language switcher to the menu.
  • A Logo, uploaded in its own card.

Further down, the Table ordering card has the switches described in chapter 4 and the Timezone. Set the timezone now: schedules, today's specials and the counter's daily totals all follow it.

Going live

Your menu starts as a draft that only you can see. The Overview page and the Visibility card in Settings both have the switch Menu is a draft. Flip it and it reads Menu is live; the badge next to your restaurant name changes from Draft to Published.

Publishing is only possible while the trial is active. If the switch refuses with "Publishing is switched off until your trial is approved", the hotel check from above is still in progress.

Suggested order

Details and timezone, then the menu (chapter 3), then tables and the ordering switches (chapter 4), then staff (chapter 5), then publish and print. Guests who scan a code before you publish see a "Menu not found" page.

3Building the menu

Categories hold dishes. Dishes can have sizes and add-ons, a date window as a special, and a switch that takes them off the menu when they run out.

Categories

Open Menu. Tap Add category, type a name such as Starters, and tap Add. The pencil renames, the trash deletes; deleting a category moves its dishes to Uncategorized, it does not delete them. Tap Reorder to drag categories into the order guests should see, then Save order.

Once you have created schedules (below), each category gets a dropdown that reads All day or the name of a schedule.

Dishes

Inside a category, add a dish. The form has:

FieldNotes
PhotoOptional but worth it. Cards with photos get the larger layout.
Name, PriceThe price is the dish's price when it has no sizes. Once you add a size group, the field reads Price (unused with sizes) and each size carries its own price.
Category, DescriptionWhere it shows, and the line under the name.
Allergens, Dietary tagsChips such as Nuts, Vegan, Gluten-free, Spicy. Guests can filter by dietary tags.
Feature this dishA star. Featured dishes carry a "Chef's Special" ribbon.
Daily specialTwo dates, From and Until. See below.
Sizes, variants & add-onsThe choices guests make when ordering. See below.

Taking a dish off the menu

Every dish card has a switch that reads On or 86. Flip it to 86 when a dish runs out. It vanishes from guests' phones at once, shows an amber 86'd badge in your dashboard, and counts on the Overview tile named 86'd. Flip it back when it is available again. Nothing else changes, so this is the switch to use several times a night.

Sizes, variants and add-ons

Only what you define is offered. A dish with no groups is ordered as it is. There are two kinds of group:

Sizes / variants · "Pick one"

The guest must pick exactly one option, and that option's price is the price of the dish. Typical: Half ₹180 and Full ₹320, or Regular and Large. Mark one option as the default with the radio button. The menu shows the dish as "from ₹180".

Add-ons

The guest may pick several, and each option's price is added to the dish. Typical: Extra cheese +20, Extra chutney +20, Butter (free). Set At least to 0 to make the group optional, or to 1 or more to require choices. Set At most to cap them, or leave it blank for any number.

Each option has its own availability switch, so you can 86 just the Large size or just one topping. The dish card in the dashboard shows a one-line summary of its groups, for example "Size: Half, Full · Extras: 3 options".

Spice level

Spice level is usually a size-style group named Spice with options Mild, Medium and Hot, all at the same price. Because it is a "pick one" group, guests have to choose, so the kitchen never has to guess.

Today's specials

Give a dish a From and Until date and it becomes a special. Between those dates it is highlighted at the top of the menu under a section named Today's specials. Outside them it is hidden entirely, so a weekend thali can live in your menu all year and appear only on the days you choose. Leave both dates empty for an everyday dish. Dates follow the timezone in Settings.

Schedules

Open Schedules to create weekly windows such as Breakfast, Lunch or Happy hour.

  1. Tap Add schedule, name it, tap the days it applies to, and set From and Until times. An end before the start makes an overnight window that counts as the day it starts, useful for a late bar menu.
  2. Save, then go to Menu and pick the schedule in the dropdown on each category it should govern.

A category on a schedule is shown only while the window is open, with no message outside it. Categories without a schedule show all day. The list on the Schedules page tells you which categories use each one, and a switch pauses a schedule without deleting it. Deleting a schedule makes its categories show all day again.

Moving a whole menu at once

Import & export moves the entire menu as one file. Export downloads what you have, including sizes and add-ons, so you can edit it in bulk or keep a backup. Import replaces the whole menu with a file: the page shows what the file adds and what it will delete before you confirm with Replace my menu. Dish pairings are cleared by an import, so export first if you want a way back.

4Tables and QR codes

Switch ordering on, tell fast_menu about your floor, and print a code for every table.

The ordering switches

In Settings, the Table ordering card has one master switch and three that depend on it. Tap Save ordering settings after changing them.

Start with Accept orders and One QR code per table. Add the kitchen screen once the floor is comfortable; it is a separate habit for the kitchen to learn.

Adding your tables

Open Tables. The box at the top takes names or ranges, separated by commas, for example:

Add tables
1-20, Bar, Patio 1-4
Will add 25: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6…
Add
  • Ranges expand: 1-20 makes twenty tables, Patio 1-4 makes four.
  • Names already in the list are skipped, so you can paste again safely. The notice reads, for example, "Added 4; 2 already existed."
  • A name can be up to 40 characters and a restaurant can have up to 300 tables.

Each row has a pencil to rename, a switch to Retire a table you are not using (its code stops resolving until you reactivate it) and a trash icon to delete it. Renaming keeps the printed code working; deleting does not.

Printing table cards

  1. Publish the menu first. A code scanned before publishing shows "Menu not found".
  2. Open QR code in the sidebar and scroll to Table codes. One card per active table is laid out, each with the table name, its QR and the line "Scan to see the menu & order".
  3. Tap Print table cards. Cut, laminate, and put each card on its table.
Codes never change

A table's code points at the table, not at its name. Rename Table 3 to Window 1 and the card keeps working. Only deleting the table breaks its card, which is why the delete button warns you.

The other codes on that page

  • The single menu code at the top opens the menu without a table. Download it as PNG or SVG, or print it as a Table tent, Sticker or Window decal. Use it on the door, on flyers, or on tables if you keep per-table codes off.
  • The review QR code opens your own review form (chapter 10), not Google. Print it on the bill.

5Your team

Give each person a login for exactly the screen they need. You create the accounts; nobody registers on their own.

Adding a staff member

  1. Open Staff and tap Add staff.
  2. Enter their name, pick a role, and enter the email they will sign in with. A safe password is generated for you; tap the refresh icon for another one. Note it down: it is not shown again.
  3. Optionally tap Upload photo to add a profile picture; it appears next to their name on the roster. You can add or change it later with the Photo button.
  4. Tap Create account and share the email and password with the person. They sign in on the normal login page and land in their app.

The role picker explains each choice: Manager, "Menu, staff, billing and the floor. Not settings." Cashier, "Billing counter and reports only." Waiter, "The waiter app: scan, approve, take and edit orders." Kitchen, "The kitchen ticket screen."

Looking after accounts

ControlWhat it does
PhotoAdds, replaces or removes the person's profile picture. JPG, PNG or WebP, up to 5 MB.
DeactivateThe person cannot sign in, and is signed out of any phone still open, but their record stays. Use it for someone on leave. Flip it back to reactivate.
PasswordGenerates a new password and shows it once. The old one stops working immediately. Use it when someone forgets theirs or leaves a phone behind.
RemoveDeletes the login. Their login stops working immediately.

The owner can manage every role. A manager can manage waiters, kitchen staff and cashiers but not other managers; those rows read Managed by the owner.

Phones and tablets

The waiter app and kitchen screen are web apps, so there is nothing to download from a store, and an update reaches every phone at once. For the best experience, add them to the home screen so they open full screen and can receive notifications:

  • iPhone: open the login page in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Notifications on iPhone only work from the home-screen version.
  • Android: open it in Chrome, tap the menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.
  • Kitchen tablet: the same, then keep the tablet plugged in with the screen set to never sleep. The kitchen screen is dark by design so it is easy on the eyes on the pass.

Pages a phone has already opened keep working through a short Wi-Fi drop. A page that was never opened shows You're offline until the connection returns.

One shared waiter phone or one each?

Both work. Each waiter with their own login gets their own notifications and the kitchen's "ready" alert goes to the waiter who approved that order. A shared phone is simpler on a small floor.

6Ordering from the table

What your guests see from the moment they scan a code to the moment they pay. Nothing to install, no account, no login.

GuestChoosedishes, sizes, add-ons
GuestPlace orderand get a code
WaiterConfirmby scanning the code

Opening the menu

Guests point their phone camera at the QR code and tap the link that appears. Two kinds of code exist:

  • A table code (printed from Tables, see chapter 4). The menu opens with a small pill at the top that reads You're at Table 4, and the waiter already knows where the order is going.
  • The general menu code (the QR studio's tents, stickers and decals). The menu opens without a table. Guests can still order; the waiter adds the table when they confirm it.

The menu itself works the way it always has: photos, allergen and diet badges, a search box that reads Search dishes…, and filter chips such as Vegan or Gluten-free. Dishes you have marked unavailable are hidden. Dishes on a schedule only appear during their hours. Date-limited specials are grouped at the top under Today's specials.

If ordering is off

When Accept orders is switched off in Settings, the menu is browse-only and no Add buttons appear. When ordering is paused, guests see an amber banner, Ordering is paused., followed by your own message or the default line "You can still browse; please ask a member of staff to order."

Adding dishes

Every dish has an Add button. A dish with no choices goes straight into the order and the button briefly turns green with Added. A dish with sizes or add-ons opens a sheet first:

  • Groups marked Choose one need a pick. If a guest forgets, the sheet says, for example, Choose a size or Pick at least one topping.
  • The price on the button updates as they choose. Add-ons show their extra cost as + ₹20, or Free.
  • The note field is for one dish only, up to 200 characters. It reaches the waiter and the kitchen with that line.
  • Quantity is set with the stepper next to the button, up to 99 per line.

Reviewing the order

As soon as one dish is in, a bar appears at the bottom of the menu: View your order with the item count and the running subtotal. Tapping it opens the Your order page.

  • Each line shows the dish, the chosen options ("Half · Extra chutney"), a note box, the price and a quantity stepper. Taking a line down to zero removes it.
  • If you allow takeaway, guests choose between Dine in and Parcel / takeaway.
  • Note for the restaurant is for the whole order, up to 300 characters: allergies, timing, anything else.
  • A line under the list explains what happens next. From a table code: "Ordering for Table 4. Your waiter will confirm it." Without one: "After you place the order, show the code on your screen to your waiter."

The button reads Place order · 3 items with the subtotal. The order is not sent to the kitchen yet. A waiter always confirms it first, so a mistaken tap costs nothing.

The order code

After placing the order the guest lands on a page headed Waiting for your waiter. Under it is a card titled Show this to your waiter with a QR code and a short code in large letters, for example K7M2QD. The code never uses 0, O, 1 or I, so it is easy to read out loud. If the waiter's camera fails, they can type it.

This page updates itself every few seconds, so guests can leave it open and watch the status change. While the order is still waiting, a Change my order button puts everything back into the cart so they can edit and place it again.

Ordering more, calling a waiter, asking for the bill

A confirmed order shows an Order more button. New dishes go on a new order, but the same table bill, so the counter sees one total for the table.

Guests who opened the menu from a table code also get two pill buttons, on the menu and on the order page:

  • Call a waiter, which turns into Waiter on the way for a minute.
  • Ask for the bill, which turns into Bill requested. The table is flagged on every staff screen and moves to the top of the counter's list.

Pressing again within a minute does not send a second request, so the staff are not spammed.

Good to know

The order lives in the guest's browser for the day. If they close the tab and scan again, their unsent items are still there. If too many orders come from one connection in a short time, the guest is asked to wait a few minutes or ask a member of staff. This protects you from pranks; real guests never hit it.

7The waiter app

A phone-first screen for the floor. Confirm what guests ordered, take orders yourself, and keep an eye on every table.

Signing in

Waiters use the same login page as everyone else, with the email and password the owner or manager gave them. After signing in they land straight on the waiter home. The bottom bar has four tabs: Home, Scan, New order and Tables. The header shows the restaurant name, a bell for notifications and a sign-out button.

Put it on the home screen

The app installs like a native one. On iPhone, open it in Safari, tap Share and choose Add to Home Screen. On Android, open it in Chrome, tap the menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen. It then opens full screen without the browser bar.

Home

Home is the waiter's to-do list. It refreshes on its own every few seconds.

  • Guests need you lists calls and bill requests: "Table 4 wants the bill", "Table 7 is calling". Tap Done once handled.
  • Waiting for approval lists guest orders that nobody has confirmed yet, with the code, table, item summary and time. Tap one to review it.
  • Open tables lists every table with guests, its order count, how long they have been seated, and the running total. A Bill requested pill shows when the guest asked to pay.

When there is nothing to do the screen says All quiet. If the owner has paused guest ordering, a banner reminds waiters that they can still take orders themselves.

Confirming a guest's order

  1. Tap Scan and point the camera at the guest's screen. When the code is read, the app vibrates, shows Got it and opens the order. If the camera is not available, type the code in the box that reads Or type the code, e.g. K7M2QD and tap Open.
  2. Check the items. Each line shows the quantity, the dish, its options and any note the guest wrote. Notes are highlighted in amber. A note for the whole order appears as Guest says:.
  3. Choose Dine in or Parcel. For dine-in, pick the table. If the guest scanned a table code it is preselected. For a parcel, type a name to call out, such as "Ravi".
  4. Tap Approve. The order goes to the kitchen and onto the table's bill. Until a table is chosen the button reads Pick a table and stays disabled.

To decline, tap Reject. The app asks for a short reason, which the guest sees on their screen. Leaving it blank is fine.

Codes expire

An order that nobody confirms disappears after two hours. Scanning it then shows No order with that code. Ask the guest to place it again.

Joined tables

A large group on several tables should pay once. In the table picker, tap more than one table. The label shows · 2 joined and the order goes on one shared bill. Every later order from any of those tables lands on the same bill.

After approval

An approved order shows a green Approved pill and four actions:

ActionUse it when
Edit itemsThe guest changes their mind. You can add, remove or change lines until the bill is paid. Save with Save changes; the bill total updates at once and the order is marked edited.
Move tableGuests moved seats. Pick the new table (or switch to parcel) and tap Move here.
Mark servedThe kitchen screen is on and the food has reached the table. Shown once at least one item is ready.
Cancel orderThe order should not be charged. It drops off the bill; the guest's screen says it was cancelled.

When the kitchen screen is enabled, each line also carries a chip: In the queue, Cooking, Ready to serve or Served. Waiters see at a glance what is safe to promise the guest.

Taking an order yourself

Some guests would rather tell you. Tap New order:

  1. Find dishes with the search box or the category chips, and tap Add. Sizes and add-ons work exactly as they do for guests.
  2. The panel This order lists what you have added, with a stepper to fix quantities.
  3. Choose dine-in and a table, or parcel and a name. Add a kitchen note if needed.
  4. Tap Send to billing. The order is created already approved, so it goes to the kitchen and the bill at once.

From a table that already has guests, open the table and tap Add an order. The table is preselected.

The table board

The Tables tab shows every table as a card and reads, for example, "5 of 12 taken. Tap a free table to seat guests."

CardMeaning

Tapping a seated table opens the bill: its orders, who placed each one, the running total and a status pill of Open, Bill requested or Paid. Paying happens at the counter, not here. A table whose guests left without ordering can be freed with Clear table.

Notifications

Tap the bell in the header and allow notifications when the phone asks. From then on the phone buzzes for:

  • New order K7M2QD, "Table 4 · 3 items. Tap to approve." Tapping opens the order.
  • Table 4 wants the bill or Table 4 is calling. Tapping opens the waiter home.

Every waiter and manager who turned the bell on is notified. If the bell says notifications are blocked, they were declined in the phone's browser settings; allow them there and try again. If the bell is missing, the owner has not set up notifications yet.

8The kitchen screen

An optional tablet on the pass. Every approved order becomes a ticket; the kitchen taps lines as they cook, and the waiter's phone buzzes when a table is ready.

Turning it on

Switch on Kitchen screen in Settings and create a login with the Kitchen role under Staff. Sign in on the tablet and it opens the board directly. Until the switch is on, the tablet reads The kitchen screen is switched off.

Reading a ticket

  • The card's border turns amber while anything is cooking and green once everything is ready.

Moving lines along

Tap a line to move it one step: Queued to Cooking to Ready to Served. Served lines are struck through. The buttons at the foot of a ticket move all its lines at once:

When the last line of an order becomes ready, the waiter who approved it gets a notification such as Table 5 is ready, "Order K7M2QD is ready to serve." The waiter can then mark the order served from their phone, or the kitchen can clear it here. Either way the ticket leaves the board.

Printing a ticket

The print icon on a ticket prints just that ticket, sized for a narrow receipt printer, in case the kitchen prefers paper for a dish or the tablet has to go on charge.

No sound on the tablet

New tickets appear on their own within a few seconds, but the tablet does not chime. Put it where the pass can see it, or pair it with the waiter's phone alerts.

9The billing counter

Every open bill by table, exactly as the waiters approved it. Mark it paid once, and see today's takings as you go.

Who sees it

The owner, managers and cashiers open Orders & billing from the dashboard sidebar. It works on a laptop at the counter or on a phone. If ordering is switched off it says so and stays empty.

The page

Three tiles at the top: Open bills, Asking for the bill, and Paid today with the day's total in your currency. The day follows the timezone in Settings, so a bill settled at 00:30 counts for the evening it belongs to.

Below them, the Open bills column lists one card per table or group:

  • Tap a card to expand its orders. Each shows who placed it, when it was approved, whether a waiter edited it, and every line with sizes, add-ons and notes.
  • Bills whose guests tapped Ask for the bill carry a Bill requested badge and sort to the top. A pill such as Calling · done clears an open call from here too.

Taking payment

  1. Collect the money however you do today: cash, card machine, UPI. fast_menu does not process payments.
  2. Tap Mark paid · ₹1,200 and confirm. The bill moves to Paid today, the tables become free on the waiter's board, and every guest at that table sees Paid. Thank you! with your Google review button.

A bill with nothing on it cannot be marked paid. Free such a table from the waiter app with Clear table.

Fixing a mistake

In the Paid today column, the owner or a manager sees Reopen on each bill. Reopening puts its orders back to unpaid and the table back on the open list. Cashiers cannot reopen; ask a manager.

Splitting and joining at the counter

A bill is always one table or one joined group. To split a group that was joined by mistake, have a waiter move the second group's orders to their own table with Move table, then settle each bill. To join two tables late, move one table's orders onto the other.

The page updates itself as waiters approve and guests order, so there is no need to refresh it.

Running the restaurant

The controls you reach for during a normal week.

10Day to day

Pausing orders when the kitchen is slammed, keeping the menu honest, collecting reviews, and what happens when the trial ends.

Pausing orders

The Pause ordering card on the dashboard Overview is for the moments when phone orders should stop but the menu should stay up: last orders, a kitchen emergency, a full house. Type a message such as "Kitchen closed, back at 7 pm", tap Save, and flip the switch. The card reads Ordering is paused.

  • Guests still see the menu, with an amber banner and your message. Their Add buttons are greyed out.
  • Waiters see a reminder on their home screen and can still take orders themselves.
  • Flip the switch back and everything resumes at once. Nothing is lost.

Pausing is different from switching Accept orders off in Settings. Pause is a daily tool for owners and managers; the settings switch is a one-time decision for the owner.

Keeping the menu honest

  • Sold out: flip the dish's switch to 86. Flip it back tomorrow. For a single size or topping, use the option's own switch inside the dish.
  • Price change: edit the dish and save. Guests who already have it in their cart pay the new price; the system checks prices when the order is placed.
  • Weekend special: set its From and Until dates once. It appears and disappears on its own.
  • New hours: edit the schedule, not every category.

Reviews

Two things share the word "review", and they are different:

Your own review form

Guests reach it from the review QR code (chapter 4). Under Reviews you see submissions on three tabs, Pending, Approved and Hidden, and choose which ones appear on your menu with Approve or Hide. Review settings lets you write the headline, add star questions, and turn the page on or off.

Google reviews

Paste your Google "Get more reviews" link in the Google reviews card in Settings. After a bill is paid, every guest at the table sees Rate us on Google on their screen. The link has to be a real Google address starting with https://, otherwise the form tells you what is wrong. Leave it empty to hide the button.

The best moment for a Google review is right after paying, which is exactly when the button appears. Ask waiters to mention it while clearing the table.

The trial and after

The trial lasts fifteen days from activation. During a needs review check, everything works except publishing; an amber banner on the dashboard reminds you. When the fifteen days end, guests can no longer reach the menu or place orders, while you and your staff can still sign in and edit everything. Contact the fast_menu team before the end date to keep the menu live.

If the connection drops

Staff screens refresh themselves every few seconds and catch up as soon as the connection is back. A guest whose phone loses signal keeps their unsent order on the phone; the order they already placed keeps its status. The waiter's order page always shows the truth: if in doubt, scan the code again.

Signing out

Every screen has a sign-out button in its header. On a shared phone or tablet, sign out at the end of the shift. If someone leaves the team, deactivate or remove their login under Staff; any phone still signed in is signed out on its next tap.

BQuestions and answers

Short answers for the situations that come up most.

A guest says they ordered but nothing shows on the waiter's phone.
Ask to see their screen. If it says Waiting for your waiter, scan or type the code; the order is there. If the page says the order was cancelled, more than two hours passed without approval. Ask them to place it again, or take it yourself with New order.
The camera will not read the code.
Turn up the guest's screen brightness, or type the code in the box under the camera. Codes never contain 0, O, 1 or I.
The guest wants to change a dish after the waiter approved.
Open the order and tap Edit items. Anything can be changed until the bill is paid. The bill total updates on its own.
Two tables want to pay together.
When approving or seating, tap both tables in the picker so they are joined. From then on, every order from either table goes on one bill. If they are already on separate bills, move one table's orders with Move table.
Two tables on one bill want to pay separately.
Move the orders that belong to the second group onto their own table with Move table, then settle each bill.
The counter marked a bill paid by mistake.
A manager or the owner can reopen it from the paid list. The orders return to the open bill.
We are slammed and cannot keep up with phone orders.
Turn on Pause ordering in Settings, with a message such as "Kitchen is full, please order with your waiter." Guests can still browse, waiters can still take orders. Turn it off when things calm down.
A dish ran out.
Open the menu in the dashboard and flip the dish's availability. It disappears from guests' phones at once. Guests who already have it in their cart are told it is no longer available when they place the order.
We renamed Table 3 to Window 1. Do we reprint the code?
No. The printed code points at the table, not its name. The new name shows everywhere immediately.
A waiter's phone does not buzz for new orders.
They need to tap the bell in the waiter app once and allow notifications. If the bell says notifications are blocked, allow them in the phone's browser settings. On iPhone the app must be added to the home screen for notifications to work.
Guests do not see the breakfast section.
Check the schedule's hours and the restaurant's timezone in Settings. A section on a schedule is hidden outside its hours, with no message.
Can guests order without any table code?
Yes. From the general menu QR they place the order and show the code; the waiter picks the table when approving. Call-waiter and bill buttons need a table code, though.
What can a guest not do?
They cannot send anything to the kitchen on their own, cannot change an order after approval, and cannot see other tables. Every price is checked again by the system when the order is placed, so an out-of-date menu on a phone cannot lead to a wrong bill.
